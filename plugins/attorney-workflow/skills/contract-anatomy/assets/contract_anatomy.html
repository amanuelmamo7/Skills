Why this game is built the way it is — behavioral design notes
  • Goal-gradient effect (Tier A/B): the career bar and 10-case session bar make progress visible; effort reliably accelerates as a goal nears.
  • Competence feedback (self-determination theory, Tier A): every case — won or lost — ends by teaching the "tell," and ranks track real skill. Absence of progress feedback is one of the strongest demotivators known.
  • Autonomy support (SDT, Tier A): you choose the mode, the pace, and when to commit. Controlling designs trigger reactance; this one never forces a reveal.
  • Open loops / Zeigarnik (Tier B): the uncracked-contract chips stay visibly unfinished — unclosed sets pull people back.
  • Fresh-start effect (Tier B): the Daily Docket resets at midnight, giving each day a clean re-entry point.
  • Peak–end rule (Tier A): the endscreen replays your sharpest call, so every session ends on its peak moment.
  • Probability overweighting (Tier B): occasional double-point briefs add variable stakes without punishing anyone.
  • Lapse-friendly streaks: a broken streak "pauses" and your best stands — self-compassion framing predicts resumption; "I blew it" framing predicts quitting.

# Mediation Process Map

The full session arc — for planning, and for explaining the process to a client who's never mediated. Mediator styles vary (facilitative to evaluative, caucus-heavy to joint-session-heavy); confirm the actual procedure with the actual mediator.

## 1. Opening and ground rules

The mediator introduces the process: they are a neutral facilitator, not a decision-maker; the parties own the outcome. Confidentiality is stated with its scope and exceptions (and rests on the signed agreement to mediate); participation is voluntary and either party may withdraw; the mediator confirms decision-makers with settlement authority are present or reachable. Introductions build the rapport the rest of the day runs on. Logistics matter more than they seem — rooms for caucusing, time constraints surfaced early, interruptions off.

## 2. Joint session — opening statements

Each side presents its perspective, concerns, and desired outcome. Openings set the stage; they don't prove the case — a scorched-earth opening costs more in the caucuses than it wins in the room. Listen for the other side's interests leaking through their positions; that's the raw material for resolution.

## 3. Caucuses

Private, confidential meetings between the mediator and each side. Nothing crosses rooms without explicit permission — which makes permission a tool: what you authorize the mediator to carry is a move. Caucuses are where interests get candid (what each side actually needs), options get tested without commitment, and the mediator reality-checks each room's view of its alternatives.

## 4. Resolution — or next steps

If agreement: terms go into clear, enforceable language and get signed before anyone leaves — momentum doesn't survive the drive home. If partial: document what's agreed, schedule the rest. If none: the mediator helps frame next steps (further sessions, arbitration, litigation), and the process still paid — each side leaves understanding the dispute, and the other room, better than it arrived.

## Closing

Thank the participants; name the progress made regardless of outcome. Disputes that don't settle in the room often settle in the following weeks on the understanding the mediation built.

# Mediation Prep

Mediation rewards a different preparation than litigation: the audience is a neutral who needs the dispute made tractable, and the goal is a resolution both sides can sign — which means preparing interests and ranges, not just positions.

> Born from: a full mediation session plan authored and run in ADR training — opening through caucuses through documentation — plus the agreement-to-mediate and mediation-statement structures used with it. Built with `skill-distiller`.

## Boundary card

**Hard lines** — this skill never:
- Attends the mediation. It drafts the statement, maps interests, and plans caucuses; counsel and the client decide and speak (`references/legal-house-rules.md`, rule 8).
- Puts caucus-only material in the joint statement. The mediation statement is written knowing the other side may see it (confirm with the mediator whether statements are exchanged or confidential — the answer changes what goes in); candid weaknesses and real ranges live in the confidential mediator letter or the internal prep, never ambiguously in between.
- Treats confidentiality as assumed. Mediation confidentiality comes from the agreement to mediate and applicable law, with exceptions — the prep names what's actually protected before anything sensitive is committed to paper.

**Judgment vs. determinism:** what to concede, what resolution is acceptable, when to caucus — judgment. The statement structure, the readiness checklist, and the internal/shareable separation are fixed.

## Phase 1 — Process readiness (the checks that decide outcomes before anyone speaks)

- **Authority:** will someone with actual settlement authority be present for each side? A mediation without decision-makers produces a term sheet nobody can sign — if authority is limited, know the limit's number before the session.
- **Papers:** agreement to mediate signed; confidentiality scope and its exceptions understood; mediator's procedure confirmed (joint statements exchanged or confidential? opening statements wanted? caucus-based or joint-session-based style?).
- **Posture:** what stage is the dispute (pre-filing, grievance step, mid-litigation), what deadlines run regardless of mediation, and what happens on no-agreement — the no-deal path is your BATNA and belongs in the prep explicitly.

## Phase 2 — The mediation statement (`assets/mediation-statement-template.md`)

Written for the mediator's actual job — understanding the dispute fast and finding the deal space: concise facts (chronological, unfavorable facts included — the mediator will hear them anyway, and candor buys credibility); each side's positions with the key authority (tiered per house rules); the interests underneath both positions; settlement history (offers made, why they failed); what resolution could look like (more than one shape); and the practical stakes of no agreement for both sides. Tone: firm on the merits, unmistakably open to resolution — a statement that reads like a trial brief tells the mediator you're not there to settle.

## Phase 3 — Interests, ranges, and caucus strategy

- **Interest map, both sides.** Behind each position, the interest it serves — compensation, precedent-avoidance, relationship preservation, vindication, operational certainty. Mediations settle on interests positions couldn't reach; the map is where creative terms come from (structured payments, procedural changes, resolution without admission).
- **Ranges.** Run the `negotiation-prep` quartet — reservation, target, BATNA/WATNA — and mark clearly what is internal-only versus authorized for the mediator's ears in caucus. What you tell the mediator privately is a tool: mediators move numbers between rooms with permission, so decide in advance what each disclosure is *for*.
- **Caucus plan.** What to raise only in caucus (weaknesses, real flexibility, decision constraints); what to ask the mediator to find out from the other room; what movement to show and in what order.
- **Paper the deal.** If agreement comes, terms go to clear, enforceable, signed writing before adjourning — a mediation that ends on a handshake reopens by Friday. Draft skeleton settlement terms in advance so drafting speed doesn't lose the moment.

## What would make this skill wrong

If the user's mediations are court-ordered with prescribed statement formats, the template defers to the local rules. If mediators consistently ignore the statements, shift weight to the confidential letter and caucus plan. If a caucus-only item ever reaches a joint document, the internal/shareable separation failed — split the files physically, as with negotiation-prep's never-disclose block.

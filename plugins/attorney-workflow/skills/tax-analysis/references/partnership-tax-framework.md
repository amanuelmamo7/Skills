# Partnership & LLC Taxation — Subchapter K Doctrine Map

The entity overlay for questions about partnerships, LLCs taxed as partnerships, and (by contrast) S corporations. Distilled as methodology from partnership-tax study texts; treat every proposition as Tier 2 until the cited section is read this session via the page index. **Staleness warning:** the underlying study materials predate 2020 — every dollar threshold, phase-out, and TCJA-era sunset (§199A, §461(l), penalty amounts) must be verified against the current Code text or a current Revenue Procedure before being stated as current.

**The organizing idea:** Subchapter K is a hybrid — the partnership is an *entity* for computing income, filing (Form 1065), elections (§703(b)), and audit, but an *aggregate* for paying tax (§701: partners, not the partnership). Nearly every hard Subchapter K question is a fight over which treatment applies, policed by three recurring safeguards: (1) allocations must track economics (§704(b)), (2) precontribution gain stays with the contributor (§704(c)), and (3) ordinary income cannot be converted or shifted through the entity (§§724, 735, 751, 1061).

## 1 — Is there a partnership, and is it taxed as one?

- **Separate entity:** a joint venture or contractual arrangement is an entity for federal tax purposes when the parties carry on a trade, business, or venture *and divide the profits* — joint profit motive is the critical factor; the intent test is whether the parties in good faith and with a business purpose intended to join in the present conduct of the enterprise (*Culbertson*). Mere co-ownership of maintained/rented property is not an entity; co-ownership plus significant tenant services is. Expense-sharing is not an entity (no joint profit).
- **Elections out:** §761(a) (investment-only, joint production/extraction, securities underwriting — participants must be able to compute income without a partnership computation); §761(f) qualified joint venture of spouses who both materially participate (each files as sole proprietor).
- **Check-the-box (Reg. §301.7701-2, -3):** state-law corporations are per se corporations; any other business entity with two or more members defaults to partnership, with one owner defaults to disregarded; any of them may elect corporate status (60-month lock after a change, with a >50%-new-ownership exception). Classification is federal, not state-law.
- **Status changes are deemed transactions:** partnership → corporation is a deemed asset transfer + liquidation (usually tax-free); corporation → partnership is a deemed *corporate liquidation* — taxable at both levels, often prohibitively. Run the deemed steps before advising any box-checking.
- **Publicly traded partnerships (§7704):** taxed as corporations unless ≥90% of gross income is qualifying passive-type income.
- **Choice of entity:** the recurring trade — LLC/partnership offers one tax layer, special allocations, debt-inclusive outside basis, and tax-free property distributions; S corporation offers SE-tax management and familiar governance but rigid eligibility and no basis credit for entity debt; C corporation offers the flat corporate rate and §1202 stock but a double tax on distribution or exit.

## 2 — Formation (§§721–723, 752, 709)

- **§721 nonrecognition** on contribution of property for an interest — both directions, no control requirement (contrast §351). "Property" is broad (cash, receivables, installment notes, intangibles, goodwill); *services are not property* (→ §6 below). Exceptions: investment-company partnerships (§721(b)); the recapture provisions are overridden (potential preserved in transferred basis).
- **Basis architecture:** outside basis = money + adjusted basis of property contributed (§722); inside basis = contributor's basis, transferred (§723). Holding period tacks for capital/§1231 assets, restarts for other property, splits proportionately for mixed contributions (§1223); the partnership always tacks.
- **Liabilities (§752) at formation:** increase in a partner's share of partnership liabilities = deemed cash contribution (basis up); decrease (including the partnership taking over the contributor's debt) = deemed cash distribution (basis down, not below zero; excess = capital gain via §731(a)). Increases and decreases in one transaction net (only the net movement counts). The trap: contributing property encumbered beyond basis triggers gain to the extent the *shifted* share of debt exceeds basis plus the contributor's share of the debt as a partner.
- **§709:** no deduction for organization or syndication costs; election to deduct up to $5,000 of organization expenses (phased out over $50,000) with 180-month amortization of the rest; syndication costs (selling interests) are never deductible or amortizable.

## 3 — Operations, reporting, and loss limits (§§701–706, 704(d), 465, 469)

- **Two-ledger discipline:** the partnership keeps a *tax* ledger (bases, taxable income) and a *§704(b) book* ledger (contributed property at FMV; capital accounts stating what each partner would take on liquidation). A partner's outside basis ≈ tax capital account + share of liabilities — the K-1's capital reconciliation and liability shares are how basis is actually reconstructed in practice; check which basis (tax, book, GAAP) Item L uses before relying on it.
- **Computation and character at entity level** (§§702(b), 703): separately state anything whose character or limitation applies partner-by-partner (capital/§1231 items, §179, charitable, portfolio income, credits); the residue is nonseparately computed "bottom line" income. Elections are made by the partnership (§703(b)) — including method (§446), §754, §179, §1033 — with narrow partner-level exceptions (§108, §617, foreign tax credit, oil & gas depletion).
- **Taxable year (§706(b)):** majority-interest year → common year of all ≥5% principal partners → least aggregate deferral; escape via business-purpose/natural-business-year (25%-of-receipts test) or §444 election with §7519 required payments. Partners pick up their shares in the year the *partnership's* year ends.
- **Basis adjustments (§705):** up for income including tax-exempt income; down (never below zero) for distributions, losses, and nondeductible noncapital expenditures — the adjustments preserve tax-exempt character and prevent double taxation of retained profits.
- **Loss limitation gauntlet, in strict order:** (1) §704(d) outside basis, (2) §465 at-risk (excludes nonrecourse debt except *qualified nonrecourse financing* on real estate from an unrelated commercial lender), (3) §469 passive loss, (4) §461(l) excess business loss. Carryforwards behave differently at disposition of the interest: §704(d) carryovers *die* (they never reduced basis); §465 carryovers deduct in full with original character; §469 carryovers release on complete taxable disposition (against gain, then other passive, then any income). Losses allowed at an earlier gate reduce basis/at-risk even if a later gate disallows them.
- **§469 essentials for entity questions:** limited partners (and LLC members lacking management rights, under the proposed-regulation line) presumptively do not materially participate; the 500-hour and related tests; rentals per se passive except real estate professionals (>half of services + >750 hours + material participation) and the $25,000 active-participation allowance (phasing out over $100k–150k MAGI); grouping by appropriate economic unit binds the partners once chosen.
- **§199A pass-through deduction:** computed at the *partner* level on the partner's share of QBI — which excludes capital gain, portfolio income, §707(a) payments, and guaranteed payments; above the (inflation-adjusted — verify) taxable-income thresholds, SSTB income is excluded and the deduction caps at the greater of 50% of W-2 wages or 25% of wages + 2.5% of UBIA, all allocated by share. The K-1 (Box 20) carries the QBI/wage/UBIA data. Verify the section's current status — enacted through 2025; check whether extended.
- **Self-employment tax:** a general partner's distributive share of trade-or-business income is SE income; a limited partner's is not (§1402(a)(13)) but guaranteed payments for services always are. LLC members fall in a gap governed by the expired proposed regulations (personal liability / authority to contract / >500 hours → general-partner treatment; service partnerships never limited) and the case law treating income *derived from services* as SE income regardless of the label (the LLP/LLC line of cases). Calibrate as unsettled where the member both invests and works.

## 4 — Allocations with economic substance (§704(b))

- Distributive shares follow the partnership agreement (§704(a)) unless an allocation lacks **substantial economic effect** — then reallocation per the partners' interests in the partnership (PIP: contributions, economic profit/loss shares, cash-flow rights, liquidation rights).
- **Economic effect — three routes:** (1) the basic test: capital accounts properly maintained + liquidation follows positive capital accounts + unconditional deficit restoration obligation; (2) the *alternate* test: first two requirements + qualified income offset, valid to the extent the allocation doesn't drive capital below the restoration obligation (counting reasonably expected distributions); (3) economic-effect equivalence (a year-end liquidation would produce the same result anyway, e.g., via state-law contribution rights). Capital accounting runs at FMV for property in and out — a property distribution therefore books the gain first.
- **Substantiality:** a reasonable possibility the allocation substantially affects pre-tax dollars. Fails when one partner's after-tax position improves and no partner's is substantially diminished (after-tax, counting outside attributes) — the *shifting* (same-year offsetting characters) and *transitory* (offset within five years) patterns. The value-equals-basis presumption (property presumed to depreciate to its deductions) validates **gain chargeback** provisions.
- **Nonrecourse deductions** cannot have economic effect (the lender bears the downside), so a four-part safe harbor deems them PIP-consistent: (1) basic test items 1–2 plus DRO or QIO; (2) allocation reasonably consistent with allocations of some significant item that does have SEE; (3) a **minimum gain chargeback** (when partnership minimum gain — gain that would result if encumbered property were surrendered for the nonrecourse debt — decreases, prior beneficiaries pick up matching income); (4) all other allocations valid. A partner's share of minimum gain functions as a deemed deficit-restoration cushion.
- **Target allocations** (allocate tax items so capital accounts hit the cash-distribution waterfall) are market practice without on-point regulations — calibrate as the better reading that PIP sustains them, not as safe-harbor certainty.
- Depreciation recapture and credits can't carry economic effect; recapture follows the partner's share of gain capped by depreciation previously allocated.

## 5 — Income-shifting safeguards (§§704(c), 704(e), 706(d), 724)

- **§704(c)(1)(A):** built-in gain/loss (book/tax disparity at contribution) allocates to the contributor on sale and via depreciation (noncontributors get tax depreciation up to book; contributor gets the remainder). The **ceiling rule** caps allocations at the partnership's actual tax items — cured (optionally) by *curative* allocations of other same-character items or the *remedial* method (partnership creates offsetting notional items; excess book basis depreciates as new property). Method is chosen property-by-property, must be reasonable, and anti-abuse recasts shifting schemes.
- **Reverse §704(c):** on a new partner's entry, revalue capital accounts (book the appreciation to the old partners) or allocate preexisting gain in the agreement — otherwise value shifts to the entrant (possible gift/compensation).
- **Built-in loss property (§704(c)(1)(C)):** the loss belongs only to the contributor; other partners compute as if basis were FMV at contribution.
- **Character taint (§724):** contributed unrealized receivables stay ordinary forever; contributed inventory stays ordinary for five years; contributed capital-loss property stays capital (to the extent of the built-in loss) for five years.
- **Family/donee interests (§704(e)):** capital-material partnerships can shift income by shifting real ownership, but the donor must first be reasonably compensated for services, and the donee's return on donated capital cannot outstrip the donor's; purchased family interests are treated as gifted.
- **Varying interests (§706(d)):** allocate by interim closing (default) or proration election; *allocable cash-basis items* (interest, taxes, service payments) spread per-day per-partner to block year-end deduction shifting; extraordinary items allocate at the moment they occur; tiered partnerships look through.

## 6 — Compensating the service partner (§§707, 83, 1061)

Three characterizations, with different stakes (timing, character, capitalization, SE tax, QBI):
- **§707(a)(1) nonpartner capacity:** taxed as an outsider transaction (partner's own accounting method controls timing; deduction/capitalization at the partnership per normal rules). Distinguished from partner-capacity acts by substance — the adviser-GP factors (services of a kind offered to others, supervision, terminable arrangement).
- **§707(a)(2)(A) disguised payments for services:** an allocation-plus-distribution recharacterized as an outsider payment where, viewed together, it lacks *significant entrepreneurial risk* (the dominant factor; the management-fee-waiver target).
- **§707(c) guaranteed payments** (for services or capital, determined without regard to income): ordinary income to the partner in the partner's year that includes the partnership year of the *deduction* (regardless of receipt); deductible or capitalizable by the partnership like any outsider payment. A minimum guarantee ("greater of $X or y% of profits") splits into distributive share (the y% amount) plus guaranteed payment (the excess). Payments determined by *gross* income can still be guaranteed payments when in substance compensation. Health premiums and retirement contributions for partners are guaranteed payments, not employee benefits — a partner is never an employee.
- **Equity for services:** a **capital interest** is §61/§83 compensation at grant (or vesting, absent an §83(b) election) at liquidation value; partnership deducts (or capitalizes) the same amount, and pre-entry appreciation stays with the old partners via §704(c) on revaluation. A **profits interest** is generally not taxed at receipt (Rev. Proc. 93-27 safe harbor, tested at grant even if unvested per Rev. Proc. 2001-43) unless the income stream is substantially certain, the interest is sold within two years, or it's a PTP interest — the *Diamond/Campbell* line explains why (speculative value; partner-capacity receipt).
- **Carried interests (§1061):** LTCG from an applicable partnership interest (investment-management businesses) recharacterizes to short-term unless the >3-year holding period is met.

## 7 — Partner–partnership property transactions and disguised sales

- Sales between partner and partnership are outsider transactions (§707(a)(1)), subject to related-party loss disallowance (§707(b), for >50% interests).
- **Disguised sales (§707(a)(2)(B)):** a contribution paired with a related distribution (two-year presumption window) is recast as a sale — the block on structuring sales as §721 + §731 nonevents; watch debt-financed distributions.

## 8 — Distributions (§§731–737, 751(b))

- **Current (nonliquidating), proportionate:** no gain except *money* received in excess of outside basis (§731(a)(1)); marketable securities count as money at FMV subject to exceptions; advances/draws are year-end distributions, so basis is measured after the year's income. Never a loss on a current distribution. Property takes carryover basis capped at remaining outside basis (§732(a)) — allocation order: cash first, then receivables/inventory (up to inside basis), then everything else; outside basis reduces accordingly (§733). No gain on property distributions regardless of value — the signature Subchapter K advantage over S/C corporations (which recognize gain at the entity on appreciated distributions).
- **Liquidating, proportionate:** gain only on money > basis; **loss allowed** only if the distribution is money + receivables + inventory alone (excess basis becomes the loss — possible even when economically ahead); otherwise the partner's whole remaining outside basis lodges in the distributed property (§732(b)) — substituted basis, so distributed property can take *more* basis than the partnership had.
- **Encumbered property:** run §752 both directions — assumption of the debt by the distributee is a deemed contribution; everyone's shrunken share of partnership debt is a deemed distribution; net at the distributee.
- **Character (§735):** distributed unrealized receivables produce ordinary income forever; distributed inventory produces ordinary income if sold within five years. Holding periods tack.
- **Partnership side:** no gain or loss (§731(b)); inside basis unadjusted unless a §754 election is in effect → §734(b) adjustment (up for distributee gain or basis step-down; down for distributee loss or basis step-up on liquidating distributions), allocated per §755.
- **Mixing bowls:** §704(c)(1)(B) — contributed property distributed to *another* partner within seven years triggers the contributor's remaining §704(c) gain/loss; §737 — the *contributor* receiving other property within seven years recognizes the lesser of (value received − outside basis) and net precontribution gain. Exceptions for distributions back to the contributor and certain like-kind swaps.
- **§751(b) disproportionate distributions:** a distribution that shifts partners' interests between hot assets (unrealized receivables + *substantially appreciated* inventory, for this section) and cold assets is recast as a taxable exchange to the extent of the shift — the ordinary-income-shifting backstop, widely criticized, with a proposed hypothetical-sale approach.

## 9 — Sales of partnership interests (§§741, 751(a), 743)

- **Seller:** amount realized includes debt-share relief (§752(d), full *Tufts* amount); basis includes the year-of-sale distributive share (the year closes as to a partner selling the entire interest). §741 capital gain/loss *except* §751(a) — consideration attributable to unrealized receivables and inventory (no substantial-appreciation requirement here) is ordinary, computed by hypothetical sale allocation; collectibles/unrecaptured-§1250 look-through applies; holding period can split. Installment reporting is limited for hot-asset gain; partnership interests are not like-kind exchange property.
- **Buyer:** cost basis outside (including debt share); inside basis unchanged (§743(a)) *unless* §754 election → §743(b) personal inside-basis adjustment = buyer's share of inside basis vs. cost, allocated per §755 — or *mandatory* when the partnership has a substantial built-in loss (inside basis exceeds FMV by more than $250,000 — verify amount current).
- **§754 is sticky:** once made it covers all future §§734(b)/743(b) events; the classic malpractice zone is a missed election on a step-up (or an unwanted one locking in step-downs).

## 10 — Retirement, death, and endgame (§§736, 708, 753)

- **§736 splits liquidation payments to a retiring/deceased partner:** §736(b) payments for the interest in partnership property → distribution rules (capital gain/loss framework, §751(b) exposure); §736(a) everything else → ordinary (distributive share if income-measured; guaranteed payment if fixed — deductible by the partnership). For a *general partner in a services partnership* (capital not a material income-producing factor), payments for unrealized receivables and *unstated* goodwill are forced into §736(a) — but a partnership-agreement goodwill provision moves goodwill to §736(b): a genuine drafting lever with opposite interests (retiree wants (b); remaining partners want (a)). Installment payouts allocate by agreement, else pro rata for fixed payments, else (b)-first.
- **Liquidation vs. sale of the interest to the other partners:** economically similar, taxed differently (§736(a) ordinary/deductible vs. §741 capital/capitalized) — characterize deliberately and document which transaction occurred.
- **Death:** the year closes as to the decedent (final return takes the short-year share); the successor's outside basis is FMV at death *minus* income in respect of a decedent (IRD — e.g., §736(a) rights, the decedent's share of cash-method receivables); no §743 step-up for IRD items.
- **Entity endgame:** actual liquidation runs the distribution rules (§736 doesn't apply to liquidating the whole firm); mergers/divisions and incorporations follow the form chosen; the 50%-sale technical termination rule was repealed (TCJA).

## 11 — Anti-abuse and judicial overlays

Reg. §1.701-2 lets the Service recast transactions using a partnership whose principal purpose is tax reduction inconsistent with the intent of Subchapter K (business purpose, substance-over-form, clear reflection), and treat the partnership as an aggregate where needed. Behind it stand the general doctrines: substance over form, step transaction (binding commitment / end result / interdependence), sham, business purpose, and codified economic substance (§7701(o), with its strict-liability penalty).

## 12 — S corporation contrast (Subchapter S, for choice-of-entity and cross-over questions)

Eligibility: ≤100 shareholders (family aggregation), individuals/estates/certain trusts only, no nonresident aliens, one class of stock (voting differences fine; straight-debt safe harbor) — §1361. Unanimous election, effective for the year if by the 15th day of month 3 (§1362); termination by revocation (>50%), disqualification, or three years of >25% passive receipts with C-era E&P; five-year re-election wait; inadvertent-termination relief. Pass-through per share, per day (§1366) — *no special allocations*; losses limited to stock basis + basis of *direct shareholder loans* (guarantees don't count until paid — the sharp contrast with §752 debt basis). Distributions: basis recovery then gain; with E&P, AAA layers first. Appreciated property distributions are *taxable* at the entity (contrast §731(b)). Entity-level taxes only for C-history corporations: §1374 built-in gains (5-year window), §1375 excess passive income. Employment-tax angle: reasonable-compensation wages, remainder of pass-through not SE income — with recharacterization risk for artificially low salaries.

## Working notes

- Route every entity question through the two-ledger frame first (tax capital vs. §704(b) book vs. outside basis): most wrong answers come from conflating them.
- The recurring computation: outside basis rolls forward as contributions + income shares + debt-share increases − distributions − loss shares − debt-share decreases. Reconstruct it from K-1 Items K and L when the client has no records, and say which capital-account basis the K-1 used.
- Verify every threshold flagged above (§709 amounts, §1061 windows, at-risk/§469 amounts, §199A thresholds, substantial built-in loss, penalty figures) against the current Code via `assets/irc-page-index.tsv` — the source texts predate several amendment cycles.
- Where the Service's position rests on proposed or expired regulations (SE tax for LLC members, §469 limited-partner definition, profits-interest liquidation-value election, §751(b) hypothetical-sale approach), calibrate accordingly and say so.
